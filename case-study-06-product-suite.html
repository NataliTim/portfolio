Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time in other products, so conventions they already know carry weight beyond the suite. The rule is to respect established conventions where they serve the task—not to copy a competitor's interface.

usability heuristics

Heuristics appear as the reasoning behind specific decisions—why a destructive action is reversible, why system status is shown during a long save—rather than as a checklist appended to a review.

Shared patterns preserve the parts of a task users already understand: how to find information, take action, confirm a change and recover from a problem. Product-specific details adapt around that common structure.

justified exception

A product used on a shop floor replaces the standard save-on-submit with autosave and an explicit offline state. The terminology, action hierarchy and recovery path stay shared; the commit model changes because the working conditions do. The exception is recorded with its rationale, scope and owner.

The playbook becomes useful when it enters everyday work: choosing an existing pattern, documenting a decision, reviewing accessibility and checking implementation against the same source.

AI features users experience

People should be able to tell when a system is assisting them, understand what it acted on, change or reject the result and recover the previous state. Transparency, control, privacy and recovery are written as interaction rules with an escalation path to a person.

assistants used internally

Internal assistance is governed by appropriate data access and accountable review: what an assistant may read, what it may produce, who signs off and what is recorded. Retrieved content never grants additional tool permissions.

A more repeatable and verifiable approach to AI-assisted delivery.

Deterministic checks can enforce explicit rules—approved components, required states, contrast, traceable sources. Generated output still requires evaluation, because the rules that matter most are the ones nobody has written down yet.

These are intended benefits with the evidence each one requires, not measured results. Numerical results are published only with their source, timeframe, sample and comparison basis.

Consistency is easy to write down and hard to keep. A rule holds when the teams using it can see why it exists, adapt it where their task genuinely differs and record that decision where the next team will find it. The harder half of the work is not defining the language but keeping it current as products change.

The same is true of the AI layer. Structured context and approved resources make assistance repeatable; they do not remove the need for someone to own the rule, review the result and update the source when the answer turns out to be wrong.
